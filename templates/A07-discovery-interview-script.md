# Discovery Interview Script

From *Forward Deployed AI Engineering* by Nasser Ghanemzadeh — Appendix A.7; see Chapter 8.

A reusable script for the first discovery interview with a line user — the person whose daily work the system is supposed to change. Adapt the bracketed sections to the specifics of the engagement. The script is calibrated for forty-five minutes. Bring no slide deck. Bring a notebook.

```
DISCOVERY INTERVIEW — LINE USER

OPENING (3 min)
  [Your name], [role at the vendor]. I'm here to learn,
  not to pitch. I don't have a product to show you.
  Everything you tell me will be shared with [sponsor name]
  in summary form, never attributed to you personally without
  asking first.

  Two ground rules: I'm going to ask you questions about
  what you actually did, not what you wish were possible.
  And if I ask something that doesn't make sense, please
  push back. I'd rather get a confused answer than a polite
  one.

CORE QUESTION (open-ended; expect 15–20 min)
  Walk me through what you did yesterday afternoon, from
  when you sat down at your desk after lunch to when you
  left for the day.

  Listen. Take notes. Ask one or two clarifying questions
  when you need to (e.g., "you mentioned the X system —
  what did you do in it specifically?"). Resist the urge
  to lead, to propose solutions, or to translate.

FOLLOW-UPS (5 min each, as relevant)
  - When you do [specific task they described], what makes
    the cases that take longer take longer? Walk me through
    one of those cases.
  - When you handed something off to [colleague they
    mentioned], what did you have to include in the handoff
    for them to be able to pick it up? What gets lost when
    you skip parts of that?
  - You mentioned [system they used]. What does that
    system do well for you, and what does it do badly?
  - The last time you got stuck on a case, what did you do?
    Who did you ask? Where did you find the answer?
  - In a typical week, what's the part of your job you
    least enjoy? What about it would you change if you
    could change one thing?

CLOSING (5 min)
  - Is there anything I should have asked that I didn't?
  - What is the part of your work you wish someone outside
    this team understood?
  - Can I follow up with one or two questions over the
    next two weeks?

POST-INTERVIEW (do alone, within 30 min)
  Write three lines:
    - One sentence on what the line user's actual workflow
      looks like (not the documented one).
    - The single biggest friction point you heard.
    - The one quote you want to remember verbatim.
  These three lines are what you'll bring to the sponsor.
```

The discipline is in the resistance. Most vendor employees, faced with a customer's description of a problem, begin mapping the description onto something the vendor's product can do. The script's structure (open-ended question, listening, follow-ups that go deeper rather than wider) is designed to suppress the mapping reflex until the real workflow has been heard.
