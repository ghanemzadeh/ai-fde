# Mock Interview Prep

From *Forward Deployed AI Engineering* by Nasser Ghanemzadeh — Appendix A.9; see Chapter 15.

A one-page checklist for the candidate preparing for the FDE customer-simulation stage described in Chapter 15. Run through this list the day before the interview, then again the morning of.

```
CUSTOMER-SIMULATION PREP

THE THREE STORIES YOU MUST HAVE READY
  1. "They asked for X, I built Y." A specific past
     engagement where the customer's stated solution was
     wrong, you diagnosed why, and you proposed and
     executed an alternative that the customer eventually
     endorsed. Three minutes max in the telling.

  2. "The bad-news conversation." A specific moment when
     you had to deliver an unfavorable result to a
     customer — a missed deadline, a failed validation, a
     scope reduction. The story names what went wrong,
     who you informed, what you took responsibility for,
     and what you proposed to do next. Three minutes max.

  3. "The scope-creep refusal." A specific moment when
     you said no to a customer's mid-engagement addition
     in a way that preserved the relationship. The story
     names the original scope, the proposed addition, the
     trade-off you surfaced, and the path you proposed
     instead. Three minutes max.

THE THREE QUESTIONS YOU MUST BE READY TO ANSWER
  1. "Walk me through your most recent customer
     engagement." Be specific. Use names of roles
     (sponsor, line user, blocker) rather than personal
     names. Tell the story the way a working FDE would
     tell it to another FDE — assume technical and
     commercial fluency.

  2. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a customer
     and were wrong." This question is the inverse of
     story #1 and harder. Have it ready. The hiring
     manager is testing whether you can hold yourself
     accountable in public.

  3. "Why this company, this role, this team — now?"
     Two sentences max. Reference a specific recent
     product launch, blog post, or hire at the company
     that informs your interest.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS YOU SHOULD ASK
  (See Chapter 15 for the full discussion.)
  - Do the FDEs ship code into the vendor's own product?
  - How is the monthly synthesis review run?
  - Walk me through your most recent engagement.
  - How is the team staffed, where does it sit in the org,
    and how does it interact with the product team?

THE NIGHT BEFORE
  - Re-read Chapter 4 (Founder-Mode) and Chapter 15
    (Interview).
  - Read the company's most recent blog post and most
    recent product release.
  - Identify one specific thing about the company you
    can name when asked why you applied.
  - Eat something. Sleep.

THE MORNING OF
  - Run through the three stories aloud, once. If you
    have a partner, run them with feedback.
  - Open your portfolio repo READMEs in tabs you can
    reference if asked.
  - Have the company's blog post and product page open.
  - Five minutes early, not fifteen. Composure under
    light pressure is the test the loop is calibrated
    for; arriving frantic compromises the test.
```

The prep is not memorization. It is rehearsal of the stories until they sound like yours, which they should, because they are.
